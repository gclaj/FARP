Concept of the operation J-FARP seizure

J-FARP Seizure.

Air assault seizure of a jump forward arming and refueling point to extend combat aviation reach into the Division deep area.

The problem operational reach

Deep targets lie beyond combat radius.

Attack aviation combat radius is bounded by fuel load. Ordnance, required reserve and time on station all draw on the same tank. When the Division deep target sits beyond that radius, the deep attack cannot be executed from the intermediate staging base.

Doctrine forward arming and refueling point

A temporary facility providing fuel and ammunition to aviation units in combat.

FM 3-04 and ATP 3-04.17 define the FARP by that purpose. Positioning Class III and Class V forward reduces turnaround distance and extends the time an aircraft can remain in the fight. Four variants are described, each trading permanence for responsiveness.

Active

Manned and operating

Equipment emplaced, crews in position, refueling in progress. The standard FARP supporting sustained operations.

Silent

Established, not operating

Emplaced and concealed. Activated only when aircraft are inbound, to limit signature until required.

Jump

Minimal footprint, air-delivered

Rapidly displaceable, with Class III and Class V delivered on the supporting aircraft. Established to support a single deep operation, then displaced or closed.

Rolling

Displaces with the force

Moves by bounds behind the maneuvering unit to keep aviation support within reach of the advance.

Purpose the Division deep area

A FARP extends operational reach.

The Division deep area is the terrain beyond the close fight, where aviation shapes enemy forces before the main effort arrives. Reaching it is a sustainment problem before it is a tactical one.

  • Reach. Refuel 200 NM forward and targets 300 NM from the ISB fall inside combat radius.
  • Station time. Aircraft arrive on the objective with a full fuel load rather than a reserve.
  • Tempo. Aircraft turn forward at the FARP in minutes instead of returning hours to the ISB.
  • Mass. More sorties on the objective within the same window. The mission statement is explicit: mass combat power in the Division deep area.

For the 101st, the J-FARP is the enabler for the large-scale, long-range air assault. The deep attack sets conditions on the follow-on objective. The J-FARP sets conditions for the deep attack.

The CONOP designates four phases by the force's primary task. The Storm Surge timeline supplies the clock: approximately two and one half hours of flight, one hour to seize, one hour of FARP operations, one hour to extract. FARP defense is not a discrete block of time. It begins at first wheels down and ends at last wheels up.

That compression is deliberate. A FARP is a high-signature, predictable target. The concept limits exposure by keeping the window short. The phases that follow address how each hour is executed.

Phase I condition setting

Select the site. Plan in reverse from it.

Key tasks: air assault planning and FARP site selection. The site must satisfy three requirements concurrently.

  • Aircraft. Dimensions and surface for a CH-47 with internal fuel aboard. Approach and departure headings clear of obstacles. Slope and dust within limits for night landing.
  • Target. Close enough to the Division deep target to preserve station time. Far enough that the fuel point is not on the objective.
  • Defense. Limited avenues of approach, cover for the security elements and an alternate site for displacement. Storm Surge plans three WETCOWs across five FARP locations.

Phase IV security posture

Security is a ground force task.

Key task: FARP Defense. ATP 3-04.17 treats the FARP as a high-value, high-signature target. The aviation unit brings minimal organic security; the ground task force provides it. The posture follows the principles of the defense applied at company scale.

  • All-round security. The FARP is approached from any direction by a mobile enemy. The perimeter must cover 360 degrees with no element split.
  • Early warning in depth. Scout observation posts forward on the avenues of approach, then the objectives, then the centre. Contact is made as far from the aircraft as possible.
  • Mutual support. Positions are sited so that any approach is under fire from two positions. The triangular patrol base is the doctrinal shape that delivers this with three elements.
  • Organic fires. The mortar section, registered on the avenues, is the company's responsive indirect fire. Aircraft on the points are the protected asset, not part of the perimeter.
  • Limited exposure. Time on site is the vulnerability. A jump FARP displaces or closes before the enemy can mass against it.

PACE. Primary Wickr over TACSAT-IW. Alternate TAK / ICE. Contingency Signal over MUOS. Emergency cell or HF.

Displacement criteria. Site compromised, or the alternate site better positioned for the next turn. The FARP moves before it is forced to.

Doctrine patrol base · TC 3-21.76

A security perimeter for a short halt.

TC 3-21.76, para 7-46: a patrol base is a security perimeter set up when a squad or platoon conducting a patrol halts for an extended period. Not occupied more than 24 hours except in an emergency. Never the same site twice. Figure 7-7 gives the shape.

  • Squads on the legs. 1st squad from 10 to 2 o'clock, 2nd squad from 2 to 6, 3rd squad from 6 to 10. Size dictated by METT-TC. Occupied the same as an ORP.
  • Machine guns at 2, 6 and 10 o'clock, each positioned to cover the front of the squad to its left. Every leg is covered by its own squad to the front and by the adjacent apex's gun firing across that front from the flank.
  • Site selection. Easily defendable for a short period, away from natural lines of drift and high-speed avenues of approach, cover and concealment from the ground, little tactical advantage to the enemy. An alternate site is selected.
  • Security in depth. OP initially positioned along the route from the security halt. R&S teams clear the site before occupation. Hasty fighting positions dug. Priorities of work set by the leader.
  • Plans required. Fire plan, alert plan, withdrawal plan with routes and a rally point or alternate base, a security system that keeps Rangers awake, and camouflage, noise and light discipline.
TC 3-21.76 Ranger Handbook · para 7-46, Figure 7-7 · ATP 3-21.813
Applied covering sectors of fire

Three objectives, one protected centre.

The objectives are the apexes of a patrol base drawn at company scale around PZ Cedar. The Storm Surge AMB is one occupation of it. The geometry is TC 3-21.76 Figure 7-7.

  • Primary sectors outward. OBJ Husky covers the northern avenue. OBJ Poodle and OBJ Bernese cover the road from the east.
  • Crew-served weapons cover the leg to their left. Husky's gun covers the Husky–Bernese leg, Poodle's the Poodle–Husky leg, Bernese's the Bernese–Poodle leg. Each leg has its own fires to the front plus a flanking gun across it.
  • Any approach is engaged twice. An enemy closing on the R3P crosses a leg under the fires of the objective on it and the gun at the adjacent apex.
  • Depth. Scout OPs forward on the avenues give early warning, as the handbook's OP along the route from the security halt does. The mortar section fires from the centre on registered targets.
  • What the FARP cannot keep. A patrol base lives on noise and light discipline and minimum movement. A FARP with rotors turning cannot. That is why the window is one turn of the attack element, with an alternate site selected in advance.

Reconsolidation H+2:00 to H+3:00

Close the site. The deep attack is on the objective.

Storm Surge Phase 5, reconsolidation, distinguishes a jump FARP from a base. The fuel point exists for one turn of the attack element and is then closed.

  • H+2:00. FARP operations complete. Refuel and rearm points recovered. WETCOW and FATCOW lift first.
  • PZ operations. Pathfinders control the pickup: chalks staged, aircraft inbound 090, staggered left, weapons tight.
  • Collapse in reverse order. Objectives fold in last, in reverse order of occupation, under mortar coverage until the mortar section itself lifts.
  • H+3:00. Reconsolidated at FCKY. Friendly end state: 100 percent accountability of personnel and equipment, FARP seizure task force validated.

Risk assessment and mitigation

A company (-), 180 NM from the ISB.

Risk to force

Distance from sustainment

A company (-) with limited sustainment 180 nautical miles from FCKY. Casualty evacuation and resupply are aviation tasks that must be planned, not assumed.

Risk to mission

High-signature fuel point

Rotors turning, fuel flowing, aircraft on the ground on a schedule. The window is the vulnerability. The concept mitigates with objective security and a compressed timeline.

Training risk

Newly qualified pathfinders

The company assumes risk by training with newly qualified pathfinders, because a Division pathfinder company is the intended recipient of this mission set.

Mitigation

Class I carried

1× DOS of CL I issued prior to W/U and 1× DOS carried per soldier on the aircraft.

Mitigation

Deliberate rehearsals

Air assault planning with 6-101 CAB, cold-load training on the supporting airframes, and leader certification prior to execution.

Mitigation

Proof of concept

Validate the task force on a training objective at full distance before the concept is relied upon for a deep attack.
